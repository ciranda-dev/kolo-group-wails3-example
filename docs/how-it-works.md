# How it works

Kolo Group is an example app. The room is built in `internal/room`: creating a room, adding and removing members, promoting a second admin, choosing who may post, reading a room from a read link, leaving a room, carrying Commits by reference and serving the encrypted tree to joiners, sending and reading messages live, storing them for the offline in the admins' segments, catching up after an absence, reading the room's history after joining it, and reopening every room after a restart. The window does not use it yet, and everything marked planned is not built.

## What is built

- **A room is an MLS group** (RFC 9420, kolo-mls). Every member device is a leaf of the tree. The creator is the room's first admin and settler; the admins' braid (kolo-braid) orders each change of the group and a SettledCommit proves that the settlers agreed to it.
- **Create, Add, Remove and Promote.** `Create` makes a room with its policy (see Posters and History). `KeyPackage` makes what a device hands to an admin. `Add` takes key packages, makes one Commit for all of them, waits until it is settled and applied, and returns one welcome for the new devices, who enter with `Join`. `Remove` removes every device of a person. `Promote` makes an online member device a second admin (see below). Each change is one new epoch.
- **Live delivery.** A message is sealed and sent on the room's gossip topic (kolo-gossip) to the members online. Every member is a gossip author because it is a leaf of the tree: the roster is read from the tree of the current epoch and of the previous one for a short window, so there is no second member list. A device publishes at most four fifths of the rate its peers accept and waits for the rest, so it is never rejected for sending too much.
- **Sealing.** The key of an epoch, K_e, is exported from the MLS epoch: `Export("kolo-group/epoch-key/v0", group id and epoch, 32)`. The room's secret, which names its topic and keys its logs, is exported once at `Create` under its own label, `kolo-group/room-secret/v0`. A message is padded and sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under K_e, with a random nonce, and its additional data names the room, the epoch, the sending device and the sequence number. The sending device signs the sealed message (Ed25519, context `kolo-group/author/v0`), so a copy of it that sits in a log proves who sent it without the gossip envelope. The id of a message is a hash of the room, device, sequence number and sealed body, so every holder of a copy computes the same id.
- **The validator.** Before a node forwards a message it checks the author's signature, the sender's rate (before any signature check), the epoch (the current one, or the previous one for a short window, or an older one the device holds when a member that was a leaf of it publishes its own message again), the body (it opens it), the number of parents and the time. A message that no honest member sends is rejected, so its sender and the peers that sent it lose score; one that is only out of date is ignored.
- **Order.** A message carries the id it answers (`ReplyTo`) and the ids its author had shown (`Parents`), the author's own previous message first. `Next` shows a message after every message it names that this device knows. A message whose parent has not arrived waits a short time, then shows. `Display` gives the same order to everyone who holds the same messages. Messages read from a segment go through the same order as live ones.
- **Admission.** `Node.Allow` is the swarm's admission rule: only devices that are leaves of a room of this node connect, except on a node that has a room with a read link or reads one, which admits any device (see Read link). A connection of another device that gets through is closed by `ServeConn`. Each room's gossip topic admits only the devices of its current epoch, and of the previous one for its window, unless the room has a read link (kolo-gossip `AllowPeer`), so a device admitted for one room never hears of another. When a device is removed, the others close their connections to it after `PastEpoch` and refuse it at once, so the Commit that removes it reaches it first; it then forgets the room: its stored keys and logs are deleted and a restart does not reopen it.
- **One connection.** `ServeConn` puts a tag byte at the start of every stream and gives each part its own view of the connection: gossip on unidirectional streams, kolo-sync on bidirectional ones, and the admins' braid on both.
- **Commits** travel by reference, for a room of 3 as for a room of thousands: see Commits by reference below.

### History

- **A key for each epoch, a link between neighbours.** Each epoch has the key K_e of Sealing. When an admin enters epoch e it writes a history link to its control log: the key of epoch e-1 sealed under K_e (XChaCha20-Poly1305, additional data the room and e, 72 bytes). A device that holds K_e opens the link and holds K_(e-1), then the link of e-1, and so on back to the oldest epoch the rule allows, so one key opens a range of epochs. A device that joins in epoch J holds K_J from its Welcome and walks back only as far as it reads. Keys it derives are kept in its store, so a restart does not walk again.
- **The rule** is chosen in `Create`, changed by `SetPolicy` and recorded in the room policy, a GroupContext extension (type 0xF003, next to the roles), which every settled Commit signs and every Welcome carries: `AllHistory` (the default), `SinceEpoch` with an epoch P, or `NoHistory`. Under `SinceEpoch` no link is written for an epoch up to P, so the key of epoch P-1 is out of reach and a joiner reads from epoch P; under `NoHistory` no link is written. A room without the extension keeps all its history. The rule is never retroactive: a link is valid only if its epoch is allowed by the policy of that epoch and its admin wrote it before any segment of that epoch or a later one in the same control log; any other link of an epoch from the join on is refused and reported with `ErrLink`. A device reads its own past by the rule it joined under, whatever the room sets later. A link of an epoch before the join that the rule of the join leaves out is dropped without a report, since the rule of its own epoch may have allowed it.
- **Changing the rule.** `SetPolicy` sets the rule for the epochs from its Commit on. Links stop at `NoHistory`, and a chain that was cut is never joined again: the rule that opens history again reaches back only to the cut, which the room records as the `Since` of the new rule (an `AllHistory` after a stretch of `NoHistory` that began at epoch E is `AllHistory` since E, and `SinceEpoch` never reaches back before the cut). A joiner then reads back to the cut and no further.
- **`History(ctx, before, n)`** returns up to n messages older than `before`, oldest first, from the epochs before this device joined. The zero id starts from the newest; any other must be the oldest message of the page the last call returned. It loads one epoch at a time, newest first: it derives the key, follows the segments that the admins' control logs name for that epoch, waits until they are complete (from the admins, other members or the pins, for at most `CatchUpWait`), reads them in the causal order of `Display`, and forgets them. A message that cannot be read (a bad signature, a key that does not open, an author the room has no certificate for, an epoch outside the rule, a segment shorter than its admin declared) is an `Event` with `Err` set and `ErrCatchUp`, never left out. When no link opens the next key, `History` returns `ErrHistory`. A message of an epoch before the join never shows in `Next`, even if an admin stored it in a later segment.
- **Joining late.** A device that joins at epoch 40 follows the admins' control logs of epoch 40, and reads epochs 1 to 39 with `History`, which follows the control logs of those epochs back, one epoch at a time, as far as it reads. The room tests do this over 40 epochs. When the links of an epoch give more than one key (a hostile admin can write a link that opens to a wrong one), `History` keeps them all, opens each message with each, and reports the disagreement with `ErrLink`.

### Segments

- **Each admin device writes two kinds of kolo-log log** (one writer each, entry keys derived from a secret every member holds, so a pin or any peer serves ciphertext that every member opens):
  - the **control logs**, one for each epoch the admin enters: the Commits (in pieces) and SettledCommits the admin saw settle (`settle.Pins` is implemented over them), the opening and closing of each segment with its final size, the tree logs it wrote, the control logs of the other admins it knows, and, as the first and last records, the control log before (`ctlPrev`) and the one after (`ctlNext`). A member follows the next one and forgets the logs of the epochs it has left past `KeepSegments`; an invite and a read link name only the current ones;
  - **segments**: every message that passed the validator, as it travelled (device, sequence number, signed sealed body). A segment is written in one epoch (a message of the epoch before that arrives late goes into it with its own epoch) and holds at most `SegmentEntries` entries; it is closed in the control log when its epoch ends or it is full.
- **Pins.** An admin pushes the logs it writes to the pins with kolo-pin `AddMany`, at most once a second for each log, under a capability that only its device key can present. A pin never joins the topic and never writes a log.
- **Members follow and serve.** Every member follows the control logs of the admins and their segments from the epoch it joined on, with kolo-sync, pulls each from the connected peer with the largest verified head, admin or member, derives the entry keys, and offers what it holds to the other members of the room and to nobody else. An admin is not needed once its logs are held.
- **Stored receipts and republishing.** A device that is no admin keeps each message it sends until a segment holds it, shows a `Stored` event when one does, and publishes it again in a new envelope after `RepublishAfter` while none does. A message sent while the admins were away is stored when one returns. A segment never holds a message twice. Receipts survive a restart and are bounded by `MaxReceipts`.
- **Catch-up.** A device that was away reads the control logs it was missing, applies the settled Commits one epoch at a time (each epoch gives it that epoch's key), and reads the segments, from the connected peers, and from the pins only when no other admin has been connected for `PinWait` or nothing has grown for `PinWait` while it is behind. While it asks the pins it pulls every log it is behind on once each `PinWait`, and it lets them go once an admin is back and it is current. An entry it cannot read, verify or open is reported as an `Event` with `ErrCatchUp` and the cause and is never shown or skipped without a report; a segment that its admin closed with more entries than anybody holds is reported after `CatchUpWait`. A SettledCommit without a quorum of settler signatures is reported once and its Commit is never fetched; one that does not apply is reported once and the next admin's is tried. A second Commit settled for one epoch is reported with `ErrEquivocation`.
- **Restart.** Rooms, MLS state, epoch keys, the authors of every device the room had, logs, receipts and the braid are kept in the stores and reopened by `Open`. An admin goes on with the segment it was writing, and writes the history link and readers' state of its epoch if a stop lost them. A restart in a moment the discovery network does not answer waits for it (each try after twice the wait before, up to `PinWait`) until `Open`'s context ends.
- **A second admin.** `Promote` publishes an offer on the room's topic; the device makes its control log and a join request for the admins' braid and publishes them; the admin admits it to the braid, the device completes the join, and a Commit changes the roles. From that epoch the device writes segments and commits as the first admin does; it is not a settler, so the first admin still settles every Commit. Only the role Admin is built.

### Posters, SetPolicy, the read link and Leave

- **Posters.** The room policy says who may post: every member (the default), the persons in `Policy.Posters`, the devices in `Policy.PosterDevices`, the admin devices with `Policy.AdminsPost`, or any union of these. A message is judged by the policy of the epoch it is sealed in and by the policy of the epoch it is judged in: the current one for a live message once the window of its epoch has passed, the epoch of its segment for a stored one; and only from a device that was a leaf of its epoch. A key of an epoch before a restriction therefore does not let a member post after it. `Send` returns `ErrNotPoster` for a device that may not post. The validator of every node returns `ErrNotPoster` and the verdict Reject, so the sender and the peers that forwarded it lose score, and an admin neither shows nor stores it. A device that reads a segment (catch-up, or `History` for the epochs from its join on) reports an entry of a non-poster as `ErrCatchUp` with `ErrNotPoster`. A member that is not a poster is a read-only member: it holds the epoch keys, so it reads every message its history rule allows, live and before its join, and cannot post.
- **SetPolicy.** An admin changes the posters, the admins' right to post, the history rule and the read link with one Commit that the settlers sign. The new policy holds from the epoch that Commit makes: a message sealed in the epoch before and in flight within its window is still judged by the old policy, a stored one by the policies of its epoch and of its segment (also after a restart, which keeps the policy of each epoch), and the history rule is not retroactive (see History). `SetPolicy`, `Promote` and `Remove` build their Commit from the view of the moment they commit, so one that waits does not undo another admin's change. `Policy()` returns the policy of the current epoch. kolo-mls accepts a Commit that changes this extension while the roles stay the same, so no library change was needed; a Commit of any size is carried by reference.
- **The read link.** A room with `Policy.ReadLink` (it needs a list of posters, or `AdminsPost`, since a reader cannot tell who is a member) gives any member a link from `Link()`. A device that calls `Node.JoinLink` with it enters the room as a reader: not a leaf of the tree and not an author, so it costs the tree nothing. The link holds the room's secret (topic and log keys), a read secret, the room's name and group id, the state of one epoch and the headers of the admins' control logs. The state of an epoch is its key K_e, the roles and policy of that epoch and the certificates of the admins and of the devices that may post; each admin writes it to its control log for every epoch, sealed under a key derived from the read secret, before any segment of the epoch. A reader follows those control logs through any peer, installs each new state, reads live messages from the gossip topic and from the segments, and reads history by the same chain of links as a member. It checks the posters and admins it was told of and no other author. It cannot post: `Send` returns `ErrNotPoster`. The link stays the same until `SetPolicy` turns the read link off and on again, which makes a new read secret: an old reader then reads nothing of the later epochs. A reader room is not kept across a restart: the device joins with the link again.
- **Leave.** `Leave` sends the admins a request, a gossip message, to remove the sender's person, sends it again every `RepublishAfter`, and returns when the room reports the removal or when its context ends. Either way the room then stops on the device: `Next` returns a Removed event and then `ErrRemoved`, and the room's state is deleted. One admin answers a request (the lowest device key of the admins that are connected) with `Remove`, so two admins make one Commit. A reader's `Leave` only stops and forgets its room. An admin device cannot leave, since the room cannot remove an admin.
- **A removed device learns it.** A device that an admin removes receives the Commit that removes it: the others close their connections to it after `PastEpoch` instead of at once, so the Commit, which it can read and verify, reaches it. Without that delay a removed device often never learned that it had been removed (in a room that had passed 8 epochs the removed device missed it in 5 of 10 runs, and with the delay in none of 15). No separate removal notice is needed: the Commit is signed by the settlers and sealed under the epoch the device is still in.
- **A segment is not forgotten while it is pushed.** An admin that keeps few closed segments (`KeepSegments`) pushed to the pins a segment that it forgot at the same time, so `Status().PinError` stayed at "no pinning peer accepted the log". The logs of a push in progress now count as not pushed yet.

### Commits by reference and the tree

- **A Commit travels as an announcement and a pull, at every size.** When a Commit settles, the admin that applied it publishes one gossip message on the room's topic: the SettledCommit (576 bytes with 4 signatures), which names the Commit by its SHA-256 and carries the settlers' signatures. The Commit itself is in the admin's control log as pieces of at most 1 MiB under that hash (a Commit that adds many members, or one of a group of 30,000 leaves at 2.4 MB, is several pieces; one of a room of 3 is one). A member pulls the pieces with kolo-sync from any connected device that holds them, admins, members or pins, checks that they hash to the name, and applies the Commit with the SettledCommit. There is no inline mode and no size threshold, so the room of 3 pays one pull of one small entry for each epoch.
- **What a member checks.** The validator of every node forwards an announcement only from an admin device, for the current epoch of this room, with a quorum of valid settler signatures (`ErrCommitRef` otherwise, verdict Reject). A member that holds the announcement still applies nothing until the Commit's pieces are all there and hash to the name in the SettledCommit; pieces that are cut short or hash to something else, from a control log of a hostile or slow admin, are passed over for the control log of another admin, or waited for. Applying checks the settlers' signatures again.
- **The announcement is a hint.** A member that missed it learns the epoch from the control logs it follows, as before; the announcement saves the wait for the SettledCommit entry to arrive.
- **The encrypted tree of a joiner.** The invite no longer holds the tree. The admin that adds writes the sealed tree of the new epoch (`settle.SealTree`, 14.5 MB at 30,000 leaves) into a log of its own, in pieces under the tree's hash, names the log in its control log, and puts the log's signed header, its length and the hash in the invite. Only the admins serve it: every other admin replicates the log and serves it to the members of the room, and forgets it when the room moves past that epoch; the admin that wrote it pushes it to the pins, which serve it too. A member that is no admin neither holds nor sends it, unless its node sets `Config.ServeTree` (off by default; it then pulls the tree of every Add, 14.5 MB at 30,000 leaves, and sends it to each joiner that asks). `Join` looks the room's topic up with the swarm (it admits any device while it runs, since the invite names none), pulls the pieces with kolo-sync from the admins that the invite names, and after `PinWait` from the pins (in a random order, so joiners share them) and from any device that answers; with no server it asks again, waiting twice as long each time up to `PinWait`, until its context ends. It checks the hash the invite carries, and lets kolo-mls open the tree with the Welcome's keys and check it against the group: a tree that fails either check never makes a room (`ErrTree`, or the library's error).
- **Retention.** The tree of an Add is kept by the admin while its Welcome is unused and while it is the latest (kolo-mls `ReleaseWelcome`); when the device the Welcome is for first sends a message, the admin releases it, and the older tree log is deleted. A joiner of an old Welcome still joins while it is unused, from the admin and the pins. The other admins, and the members that serve, hold only the replica of the newest tree log.

### A room of 100,000 readers and 3 posters

The code is the same as for 3 members: there is no mode and no threshold. Readers hold no leaf, so the MLS tree holds the members only, and a reader costs the room one state record for each epoch, written once for all of them. What is measured (see [limits](limits.md), `TestMeasureReaders`): the policy is 70 bytes for one poster and a read link, 3,238 bytes for 100 posters and 320 KB for 10,000; the state of an epoch with one author is 514 bytes sealed (267 bytes for each author); a link is 779 bytes; a reader opens and installs a state in 4 microseconds. What is not measured: any run with more than 64 devices of this app, so the load of 100,000 readers on the members and admins that serve the logs, the gossip mesh at that size (kolo-gossip's simulator went to 30,000 nodes), and the time a reader needs to catch up many epochs. A reader reads the control logs and segments from any connected peer, readers included, and the pins, so the admins are not the only source, but how well that scales is UNVERIFIED. A reader's Sybil defense is kolo-gossip's peer score only.

## What is not built

- **Settlers** (planned): `Promote` to `Settler` returns `room: not implemented`.
- **Persistence of a reader room** (not built): a room joined with `JoinLink` is forgotten at a restart.
- **The policy of the epochs before the join** (not built): a joiner holds no policy of the epochs before it joined, so `History` does not check the posters of those messages; the admins that stored them had refused the messages of non-posters.
- **Authors that left before the join** (not built): a joiner holds the certificates of the devices that are, or were since it joined, in the room. A message of a device that left earlier is reported by `History` with `ErrUnknownAuthor`, since no library gives the credentials of past members.
- **A tree log written by a device that is no admin** (not built): only the admin that adds writes the tree log of the epoch it makes; the other admins and the members that serve replicate it.
- **Hints of new heads on gossip** (not built; the kolo-sync watch already tells every connected peer when a log grows).
- **The window, invites and `-headless`** (planned).
- **Wake-up of offline devices** (planned, needs the mobile apps).
