# Security

The app is experimental and has had no security review. The room, its segments, its catch-up, the history for new members, the posters policy, the read link and leaving are built and the points below say what they do and what they do not do. Points marked planned are not built.

## What the live room does

- **Message secrecy**: a message is sealed under K_e, a key every member of the epoch derives from the MLS exporter (RFC 9420 section 8.5), with XChaCha20-Poly1305 and a random 24-byte nonce. Only devices that are leaves of that epoch hold K_e. Message sizes are padded to a Padme length, so the exact length of a text is not visible.
- **Authorship**: the gossip envelope is signed by the sending device and the device is a leaf of the tree, with a certificate chain to its person (kolo-id). The sending device also signs the sealed message itself, over its room, epoch, device, sequence number, nonce and ciphertext, so the copy that an admin stores shows who sent it without the envelope. The additional data of a sealed message holds the device and the sequence number, so a member cannot copy a sealed body into an envelope of its own or of another sender: its signature does not verify, and the message is rejected. A member cannot sign as another member.
- **Stored messages**: an admin is trusted to keep every message, never to say who wrote one or what it says. A reader checks each stored message as it checks a live one: the author's signature, that the author is or was a device of the room, and that the message opens under the key of its epoch. An entry that fails a check is reported with `ErrCatchUp` and the cause, not shown and not skipped without a report. An admin that stores forged, unreadable or unsigned entries, an entry sealed under an epoch after its segment's, a SettledCommit without a quorum of settler signatures or one that does not apply, a segment shorter than it declared or a second history of a log it signed is reported and does not change what the room shows or stop the room: an entry of a later epoch is reported with `ErrWrongEpoch` and the entries after it are read, a SettledCommit without its quorum is reported once and its Commit is never fetched, one that does not apply is skipped for the next admin's, a second history is refused by kolo-sync and the member keeps what it verified.
- **The logs**: every log is signed by the device that writes it and checked by every reader against that device's key, whoever serves it (kolo-log, kolo-sync). A pin keeps ciphertext and never joins the topic; it accepts a log only from the key that signed it. The entry keys of the room's logs derive from a secret that every member holds, so the pin cannot read them and every member can, whoever serves the log. Members serve the logs they hold only to the members of the room that owns them.
- **Catch-up**: a Commit read from a control log is applied only with the settlers' signatures, as one from gossip is, so an admin cannot make a member apply anything the settlers did not settle. The group, epoch, braid and quorum of the SettledCommit are checked before its Commit is fetched.
- **Removal**: a removed device does not hold the key of any later epoch, so it reads nothing sent after its removal. The members refuse it as soon as they apply the settled Commit: the swarm refuses a new connection from it, they close the connections it has after the short window of the previous epoch (10 seconds by default), so that the Commit reaches it and it learns it was removed (it pulls that Commit from the control logs, which the members serve it for the same window, and then nothing more), and its messages are refused once that window ends. During that window a message it seals under the old key is still accepted, since it cannot be told from one in flight.
- **Applying a Commit** needs the signatures of a quorum of settlers (kolo-mls). The announcement of a Commit on the room's topic is accepted for forwarding only from an admin device, only for the current epoch, and only with a quorum of valid settler signatures (`ErrCommitRef` otherwise); it holds no Commit. A Commit is pulled from the control logs as pieces and used only when every piece is there and the whole hashes to the name in the SettledCommit, so an admin or a peer that serves a cut or another Commit under that name is passed over for another admin's log, and the settlers' signatures are checked before the Commit is fetched, on both paths, and again when it is applied.
- **The tree of a joiner**: the sealed tree is encrypted under keys that only a holder of the Welcome derives, and each sealer's copy is bound to its device key, the room and the epoch. The invite carries the hash of the sealed tree and the signed header of the log that holds it; the joiner checks the hash, and kolo-mls opens the tree with the Welcome's keys and checks it against the group, so a peer, a pin or an admin that serves another tree, or a cut one, is refused and reported with `ErrTree`. Entries of the log are verified against the head the admin signed, as every log is.
- **Admission**: only member devices connect, and `ServeConn` refuses the others. A node that has a room with a read link, or reads one, admits any device (see the read link below). Each room's gossip topic admits only the devices of its current epoch, and of the previous one while its window lasts (kolo-gossip `AllowPeer`); a room with a read link, or read with one, admits any device. A device that a node admits for one room is never told the topic of another, closed room of that node and gets none of its envelopes.
- **Posters**: the room policy says who may post, and it is a GroupContext extension that every settled Commit signs. A message is judged by the policy of the epoch it is sealed in and by the policy of the epoch it is judged in: the current one for a live message (its own while it is the current epoch or the previous one in its window, so a message in flight when a Commit applies is not lost), and for a stored one the epoch of its segment (its own when the segment is of that epoch or the next). A device that was not a leaf of the epoch a message names never passes. So a member that held the key of an epoch before a restriction cannot post with it after the window of that epoch. Every node refuses a post by a device that may not post (`ErrNotPoster`, verdict Reject, so the sender and the peers that forwarded it lose score), an admin neither shows nor stores it, and a reader of a segment reports it. The policy is enforced by the nodes, not by the cryptography: every member holds the epoch key, so a member that may not post can seal a message that opens and sign it as itself, and honest nodes refuse it; a hostile admin can store one in a segment, and a reader of an epoch from its own join on reports it. A joiner holds no policy of the epochs before it joined, so `History` does not check posters for those. A message that a poster signed is authenticated as the poster's, whoever stores it.
- **Leaving**: a request to leave is one gossip message from a member, accepted from a device that is a member now; an admin removes the sender's person with the same Commit that `Remove` makes, so a member can have only its own person removed. The request names no epoch, so against a replay it relies on gossip's checks of duplicates and age.
- **Flood**: each device may send a bounded number of messages per second to a room, ten by default, and no more than ten at once. A message over the rate is rejected, which lowers the sender's score at every node that sees it.
- **Replay**: gossip drops a message it has seen. A message of an epoch this node holds no key of, of an epoch older than the node's join, or one whose additional data differ, is not accepted; a message of an older epoch is accepted only as the next point says. A repeated message shows once.
- **Bounds**: every size, queue and wait of the room is bounded and can be raised (see [limits](limits.md)).

## What it does not do

- **History keeps keys, and one key opens a range**: whoever holds a room's epoch key reads every message of that epoch. Each member keeps the key of every epoch it was in, in the app's store, so a stolen device opens everything it could read, and the segments it holds. MLS deletes used secrets so that old messages stay closed (RFC 9420 section 9.2); the app keeps the epoch keys on purpose, so that protection does not cover the room's messages.
- **The chain of history links costs forward secrecy of the past** (the owner's default is all history): the key of epoch e opens the link that holds the key of epoch e-1, and so on back to the first epoch the room's rule allows. Anyone who holds one epoch's key, a device that joined in it, a stolen device, or a member that passes the key on, reads every message of the room before it, from the first epoch the rule allows up to that epoch, as soon as it holds the segments, which the admins' logs and the pins serve as ciphertext to anyone who knows their ids. A room that wants a past to stay closed chooses `SinceEpoch` or `NoHistory` when it creates the room, or sets it with `SetPolicy` for the epochs from then on; a link that was not written is never written afterwards, and a rule that opens history again reaches back only to the cut.
- **A removed member keeps what it already read, and nothing after**: removal stops future messages only. The removed device holds the keys of the epochs it was in and may keep the messages it read, and the history of those epochs stays open to it. It does not hold the key of the epoch that removed it or of any later one, and every later link is sealed under a key it lacks, so it reads nothing sent from that epoch on; the room's tests show that it opens none of those links. A device that joins later reads the history of the room, including the epochs a removed member was in.
- **The links are written by admins and checked by readers**: an admin writes the link of each epoch it enters, before any segment of that epoch, and a reader refuses a link written after a segment of its epoch or a later one, and a link for an epoch the room's rule leaves out (`ErrLink`, reported). A link only proves that its writer held the key of its epoch, which every admin of that epoch does, so a hostile admin can write one that opens to a wrong key. `History` keeps every key that the links of an epoch give (the one most links agree on first), opens each message with each of them, follows the chain from each, and reports a disagreement with `ErrLink`; a wrong key therefore costs a few failed openings and never hides what an honest admin's link opens. When no link of an epoch opens, `History` returns `ErrHistory` and reads nothing further back. With one admin, or when every admin of an epoch lies, the messages sealed under the right key do not open and are reported. An admin holds the keys of every epoch it was in, so it can read every message of them, and can leave a link unwritten (another admin's link for the epoch is then used). An admin that stops between applying a Commit and writing the link of the new epoch writes it when it opens the room again.
- **The history rule is part of the room's state**: it is a GroupContext extension that every settled Commit signs and every Welcome carries, so an admin cannot give a joiner another rule than the settlers agreed.
- **Authors of old messages**: a stored message is checked against the certificates of the devices the joiner knows, those of the room since it joined. A message of a device that left before is reported by `History` with `ErrUnknownAuthor` and not shown, since no certificate can show that the device was a member.
- **Who holds and serves the tree**: the admins and the pins, and a member only when its node sets `Config.ServeTree`. A device that does not serve holds no copy of the tree log, so the sealed tree of an Add (ciphertext, opened only with the Welcome's keys) is on fewer devices, and a joiner needs an admin or a pin online, or a member that opted in. A device serves a tree log only to the admins, to a device whose Welcome into its epoch it made, to a member that joined in its epoch and, while that epoch is the current one, to the members (a member that serves trees replicates it then); a reader of the read link, a removed device and a member after the epoch are refused. A member can still pull the tree log of the current epoch again and again: that is bounded only by kolo-sync's byte budget for each peer (32 MiB a second and a burst of 256 MiB by default, chosen, not measured). A pin serves the logs it holds by its own rules (kolo-pin).
- **A join admits any device while it pulls the tree**: the invite names the admins but no other serving device, so `Join` looks the topic up and lets any device that the swarm connects talk to this node until the tree is in. What such a device can do is bounded: serve pieces of the tree log (checked by hash and by the Welcome), pull the logs this node serves to any device (those of a room with a read link), speak gossip for topics whose rooms admit any device, and hold a connection. The topics of this node's closed rooms are not announced to it and it is not let into their meshes. Nothing it says is believed without a check.
- **A Welcome counts as used when its device first sends a message**: the admin then lets the older tree go. A device that joins and never sends keeps the tree on the admin that added it; that is storage, not exposure, since the sealed tree opens only with the Welcome's keys.
- **The invite is trusted as delivered** (not preventable here): the welcome an admin returns holds the room's topic secret and name next to the MLS Welcome. The MLS parts are authenticated; the topic secret is not. It must reach the new device over an authenticated, encrypted channel (kolo-invite). A swapped secret only sends the joiner to a topic that nobody else uses.
- **Metadata**: the mesh peers of a topic and the nodes that serve its discovery records see who takes part and when messages are sent; the envelope names the author's person and device. The topic is a hash of a secret that members hold, so a discovery node cannot link it to the room's name.
- **A device that is offline needs an admin or a pin that stored the message**: a room with no always-on admin and no pin delivers to the members online only, and a message waits as a receipt at its author until an admin returns. A message is lost if its author loses the device before an admin stored it.
- **An admin can withhold**: it can leave a message out of its segments, and another admin's segment or the author's republishing then stores it. With one admin the room trusts it for completeness.
- **What a pin and the other peers learn**: which owner keys and log ids a room has, how many entries they hold and when they grow, who pulls them and when. From the sizes: the tree log of an Add gives the number of leaves (14.5 MB at 30,000), the size of a Commit the number of members it adds or removes, a new segment or a new control log marks an epoch change, and the size of a readers' state the number of admins and posters. A removed member that kept the room's secret can still read the control logs of later epochs, which hold the room's Commits, and see the size of its segments; it cannot open a message, which is sealed under a key of an epoch it was not in. Any peer that knows an owner key and a log id may pull a pin's copy, which is ciphertext.
- **Old-epoch messages from members**: the validator accepts, from a device that is a member now and was a leaf of that epoch, a message of an older epoch whose key the node holds, if both that epoch's policy and the current one let it post, because the author of an unstored message publishes it again. A member could use this to send a message that looks old; the time inside it is the author's own, as for every message, and the order of messages does not depend on it. A message that an author sealed before a restriction and publishes again after it is refused, which is what the restriction asks.
- **Stored messages are checked against the devices the room has had and the first epoch each was a leaf in, not against every epoch**: a hostile admin could store a message that a removed device wrote under the key of an epoch it was in, with any time inside it, and a reader cannot tell it from an old message of that device.
- **One settler is trusted not to fork the room**: a room has one settler, its creator, so the quorum is one signature. A settler that signs two Commits for one epoch can give each to a part of the room; a member that sees both (an announcement, the control logs, or a late SettledCommit of the epoch before) reports it once with `ErrEquivocation`, but the room does not heal the fork.
- **Promotion is public to the room**: the offer, the request to join the admins' braid and its welcome travel on the room's topic and every member sees them; they hold keys and ids that are public, no secret.
- **Revocations** (planned): the roster holds no revocation of a device certificate yet; removing a device from the room is how it is stopped.
- **Passing keys outside the protocol** (not preventable): a member can always show a message or a key to someone else.
- **A public read link gives no secrecy against anyone who holds the link.** The link holds the room's secret and a read secret, and the admins write the epoch keys sealed under a key derived from the read secret, so whoever has the link reads every message of the room, live and in its history, from the epoch of the link on and as far back as the history rule allows. The link can be copied and passed on, and one that has been given out cannot be taken back from a device: `SetPolicy` turns the read link off and on again for a new read secret, and a device with the old link then reads nothing of the later epochs, but keeps what it read. End-to-end secrecy of the room's messages therefore holds against nobody who is a reader, and it holds against the devices that have neither the link nor the room's keys (the pins, the nodes that serve discovery records, a peer that only forwards ciphertext). What the encryption still gives a room with a read link is authorship and integrity: a reader accepts a message only from the admins and the posters its states name, with the signature of the device and a certificate chain to the person, so a reader cannot be made to take a message that a poster did not write, and a stored message shows who signed it. Whoever gives out the link has decided that the room is public.
- **Removal in a room with a read link**: every member holds the read secret, since it is in the GroupContext, so every removed member of a room with a read link can go on reading as a reader, whether or not it was given the link, and a node that admits any device does not close its connections. To stop it, remove the person and then turn the read link off and on again with `SetPolicy`, which makes a new read secret that the removed device does not hold.
- **What a reader trusts**: the admins whose control logs the link names, and the admins that the roles in their states name. An admin writes the roles, the policy and the posters' certificates of each epoch for the readers, so an admin that lies to readers can make them accept another poster; the members, which check the tree, cannot be made to. A reader does not see the members that do not post, and it is not a leaf: it is not in the tree, cannot be removed alone, and sees only what the link lets it see.
- **Sybil and spam with a read link**: a node that has a room with a read link, or reads one, admits any device, and so does any node while a `Join` pulls its tree, so its defense against devices that send nothing but noise is kolo-gossip's peer score and the limits on connections. Such a device gets a connection, kolo-sync for the logs this node serves to anyone (those of the rooms with a read link) and gossip for the topics of those rooms only: the topics of the node's closed rooms are never announced to it and it is never let into their meshes (kolo-gossip `AllowPeer`; `TestReadLinkHolderGetsNothingOfAnotherRoomOfTheNode`). Before that, such a device learned the topics of the node's other rooms and received their sealed envelopes, with the author keys, timing and sizes. It still learns that the node is online and takes part in a public room.
- **Always-on admin** (planned): a device that runs headless as an admin holds admin and MLS keys, so it must be kept as safe as a person's own device.
