# Security

The app is experimental and has had no security review. The room, its segments and its catch-up are built and the points below say what they do and what they do not do. Points marked planned are not built.

## What the live room does

- **Message secrecy**: a message is sealed under K_e, a key every member of the epoch derives from the MLS exporter (RFC 9420 section 8.5), with XChaCha20-Poly1305 and a random 24-byte nonce. Only devices that are leaves of that epoch hold K_e. Message sizes are padded to a Padme length, so the exact length of a text is not visible.
- **Authorship**: the gossip envelope is signed by the sending device and the device is a leaf of the tree, with a certificate chain to its person (kolo-id). The sending device also signs the sealed message itself, over its room, epoch, device, sequence number, nonce and ciphertext, so the copy that an admin stores shows who sent it without the envelope. The additional data of a sealed message holds the device and the sequence number, so a member cannot copy a sealed body into an envelope of its own or of another sender: its signature does not verify, and the message is rejected. A member cannot sign as another member.
- **Stored messages**: an admin is trusted to keep every message, never to say who wrote one or what it says. A reader checks each stored message as it checks a live one: the author's signature, that the author is or was a device of the room, and that the message opens under the key of its epoch. An entry that fails a check is reported with `ErrCatchUp` and the cause, not shown and not skipped without a report. An admin that stores forged, unreadable or unsigned entries, a SettledCommit that does not apply, a segment shorter than it declared or a second history of a log it signed is reported and does not change what the room shows or stop the room: a SettledCommit that does not apply is skipped for the next admin's, a second history is refused by kolo-sync and the member keeps what it verified.
- **The logs**: every log is signed by the device that writes it and checked by every reader against that device's key, whoever serves it (kolo-log, kolo-sync). A pin keeps ciphertext and never joins the topic; it accepts a log only from the key that signed it. The entry keys of the room's logs derive from a secret that every member holds, so the pin cannot read them and every member can, whoever serves the log. Members serve the logs they hold only to the members of the room that owns them.
- **Catch-up**: a Commit read from a control log is applied only with the settlers' signatures, as one from gossip is, so an admin cannot make a member apply anything the settlers did not settle.
- **Removal**: a removed device does not hold the key of any later epoch, so it reads nothing sent after its removal. The members stop admitting it as soon as they apply the settled Commit: its connections are closed, the swarm refuses it, and its messages are refused once the short window of the previous epoch (10 seconds by default) ends. During that window a message it seals under the old key is still accepted, since it cannot be told from one in flight.
- **Applying a Commit** needs the signatures of a quorum of settlers (kolo-mls). A message of the room that claims to carry a Commit is accepted for forwarding only from an admin device and only for the current epoch.
- **Admission**: only member devices connect, and `ServeConn` refuses the others.
- **Flood**: each device may send a bounded number of messages per second to a room, ten by default, and no more than ten at once. A message over the rate is rejected, which lowers the sender's score at every node that sees it.
- **Replay**: gossip drops a message it has seen. A message of another epoch, or one whose additional data differ, is not accepted. A repeated message shows once.
- **Bounds**: every size, queue and wait of the room is bounded and can be raised (see [limits](limits.md)).

## What it does not do

- **History keeps keys**: whoever holds a room's epoch key reads every message of that epoch. Each member keeps the key of every epoch it was in, in the app's store, so a stolen device opens everything it could read, and the segments it holds. A removed device deletes them. MLS deletes used secrets so that old messages stay closed (RFC 9420 section 9.2); the app keeps the epoch keys on purpose, so that protection does not cover the room's messages.
- **The invite is trusted as delivered** (not preventable here): the welcome an admin returns holds the room's topic secret and name next to the MLS Welcome. The MLS parts are authenticated; the topic secret is not. It must reach the new device over an authenticated, encrypted channel (kolo-invite). A swapped secret only sends the joiner to a topic that nobody else uses.
- **Metadata**: the mesh peers of a topic and the nodes that serve its discovery records see who takes part and when messages are sent; the envelope names the author's person and device. The topic is a hash of a secret that members hold, so a discovery node cannot link it to the room's name.
- **A device that is offline needs an admin or a pin that stored the message**: a room with no always-on admin and no pin delivers to the members online only, and a message waits as a receipt at its author until an admin returns. A message is lost if its author loses the device before an admin stored it.
- **An admin can withhold**: it can leave a message out of its segments, and another admin's segment or the author's republishing then stores it. With one admin the room trusts it for completeness.
- **What a pin and the other peers learn**: which owner keys and log ids a room has, how many entries they hold and when they grow, who pulls them and when. A removed member that kept the room's secret can still read the control logs of later epochs, which hold the room's Commits, and see the size of its segments; it cannot open a message, which is sealed under a key of an epoch it was not in. Any peer that knows an owner key and a log id may pull a pin's copy, which is ciphertext.
- **Old-epoch messages from members**: the validator accepts, from a device that is a member now, a message of an older epoch whose key the node holds, because the author of an unstored message publishes it again. A member could use this to send a message that looks old; the time inside it is the author's own, as for every message, and the order of messages does not depend on it.
- **Stored messages are checked against the devices the room has had, not against the exact epoch**: a hostile admin could store a message that a removed device wrote under an epoch key it kept, with any time inside it, and a reader cannot tell it from an old message of that device.
- **Promotion is public to the room**: the offer, the request to join the admins' braid and its welcome travel on the room's topic and every member sees them; they hold keys and ids that are public, no secret.
- **Revocations** (planned): the roster holds no revocation of a device certificate yet; removing a device from the room is how it is stopped.
- **Passing keys outside the protocol** (not preventable): a member can always show a message or a key to someone else.
- **Read link** (planned): a room with a read link gives integrity and authorship, not secrecy. Anyone with the link reads.
- **Always-on admin** (planned): a device that runs headless as an admin holds admin and MLS keys, so it must be kept as safe as a person's own device.
- **Sybil and spam in a room with a read link** (planned): the topic then admits any device, so its defense is the gossip peer score only.
